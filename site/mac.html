Using it on a Mac

Seven steps. The first two are the ones macOS makes you do for any app that did not come from the App Store.

1

Pick the right file, then drag it to Applications

Apple menu, About This Mac. If the chip line says Apple M1, M2, M3 or newer, take the Apple Silicon download. If it says Intel, take the Intel one. The wrong one simply will not open.

Open the download. A window appears with Coco Whisper, a shortcut to Applications and READ ME FIRST.txt. Drag Coco Whisper onto Applications.

Had the old Coco Whisper? Quit it from the menu bar and drag it from Applications to the Trash. Your settings and the speech model carry over to Coco Whisper.
2

Allow it once in Privacy and Security

Open Coco Whisper from Applications. macOS says it could not verify the app, because it is not notarised with Apple. Click Done.

Go to System Settings, Privacy and Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to Coco Whisper.

Stuck on a message saying the app is damaged? Open Terminal and paste this, then try again:
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Coco Whisper.app"
3

Give it the two permissions it needs

Accessibility, so it can notice the dictation key and paste the text for you. The app opens a small window for this: click Ask macOS, then Open System Settings, and switch Coco Whisper on. The app closes and reopens by itself.

Microphone, so it can hear you. macOS asks the first time you dictate, just say yes.

You only do this once. Every version is signed with the same certificate, so updates keep the permission. To update, drag the new Coco Whisper onto Applications and choose Replace.
4

Find the microphone icon in the menu bar

The app has no window and no dock icon. It lives at the top right of your screen, in the row with the clock, wifi and battery.

If your menu bar is crowded, the icon can be hidden behind the notch on some MacBooks. Holding Command and dragging menu bar icons rearranges them.

5

Click where you want to write, then hold the key

Click into the message box first, in Slack, Gmail, the ATS, anywhere. Then hold Right Command and talk.

This pill appears at the bottom of the screen while it hears you. Captured on Windows, it is the same pill on a Mac.

Let go when you finish. A second or two later the text appears where the cursor was.

6

The menu

Click the microphone icon in the menu bar.

Hold Right Command to dictate
Language  English, Portuguese, Tagalog, Taglish
Copy last transcript
Settings
Open log folder
Quit

Switching language takes effect immediately, no restart.

7

Settings, if you want to change the key

Open Settings from that menu. The keys you can choose from on a Mac:

Right CommandRight OptionRight Control Right ShiftF9Control + Shift Control + SpaceOption + Space

Mode swaps holding the key for pressing once to start and once to stop, which is easier for long dictation.

Sound volume sets how loud the start and stop tones are. It starts quiet, and the Test button plays it at the level you picked.

Word replacements is for words it always gets wrong. One per line, written as spoken = written.

The settings window, captured on Windows. The Mac one has the same rows in the same order, drawn with Mac controls.

When something is off

I hold the key and nothing happens

Nine times out of ten this is the Accessibility permission from step three. Check System Settings, Privacy and Security, Accessibility, and make sure Coco Whisper is switched on. The menu bar icon also has a Permissions item that checks it for you.

Right Command does nothing but another key works

Tell the team, that is worth knowing. In the meantime pick a different key in Settings, Control plus Shift is a good second choice.

macOS says the app is damaged and cannot be opened

That is Gatekeeper, not a corrupted download. Use the Terminal command in step two, then open it with a right click.

It heard nothing

Open Settings and choose your microphone by name instead of leaving it on system default. Also check System Settings, Privacy and Security, Microphone.

It is slow

Open Settings and pick a smaller model. Apple Silicon handles the larger ones comfortably, an older Intel Mac should stay on Small.

On Windows instead? Read the Windows guide. This app is newer on the Mac than on Windows, so tell the team anything that looks wrong.
